Strategy and Rules Report

What the local dashboard actually uses to estimate a target touch, what was tested, and what was rejected.

Scope: This report documents the code in stock_details.html, indicators.js, backtest.py, walkforward_results.json, and the Pine indicators in this workspace.
Instrument: ETHUSDT. Live source: Binance Vision through the local Python proxy. Base data: 1-minute OHLCV candles.
Important: This is decision-support software, not a guaranteed prediction, trading signal, investment recommendation, or execution system. A probability means historical frequency under similar conditions; it does not mean the target must be reached.

Prepared from the implemented repository logic. The live dashboard refreshes price every 5 seconds and refreshes its 500-candle history approximately every 60 seconds while streaming.

1. Executive Summary

The dashboard does not use a neural network or a deterministic forecast price. Its primary live engine estimates the probability that ETH will touch a user-entered target level within each minute from the current point through the end of the current 15-minute window.

  1. Fetch the current ETHUSDT price.
  2. Fetch up to 500 recent 1-minute candles.
  3. Measure recent momentum, current volatility, recent volume, and RSI(14).
  4. Search historical candles for similar momentum and volatility conditions.
  5. Measure whether the requested percentage move was touched within 1 to 15 minutes after those historical points.
  6. Weight recent historical examples more heavily, blend matched examples with all examples, and calibrate the resulting probability.
  7. Display a minute-by-minute probability, median move, 90th-percentile move, and a simple Can Cross decision.
What is live: target-crossing probability, historical move statistics, trend label, volatility regime, volume label, RSI value, and a gated RSI reversion label.
What is not live: the EMA9/EMA21 + MACD strategy, Bollinger strategy, raw momentum bias, Pine Script alerts, order execution, stop-loss management, position sizing, and news/sentiment analysis.

3. Primary Live Strategy: Historical Target-Touch Probability

3.1 Recent momentum classification

The dashboard computes the return from the open of the oldest candle in the last 20 candles to the close of the newest candle:

momReturn = (last close - first open) / first open * 100 trend = UP if momReturn > 0.02% DOWN if momReturn < -0.02% FLAT otherwise

This trend is descriptive context. It is not itself the final target probability.

3.2 Volatility regime

For every eligible historical point, the code computes a 20-bar range-based ATR percentage:

ATR20(i) = average over 20 bars of ((high - low) / close) * 100 ATR ratio = current ATR20 / average historical ATR20 Hot if ATR ratio >= 1.5 Normal if 0.8 <= ATR ratio < 1.5 Quiet if ATR ratio < 0.8

For a matched historical sample, its ATR must be between 0.5x and 2.0x the current ATR. This prevents comparing an unusually calm historical period with an unusually volatile current period, or vice versa.

3.3 Historical matching

For each horizon m = 1..15, every historical point after the 20-bar warmup is evaluated. A point is a matched sample when:

  • Its 20-bar momentum label equals the current trend label.
  • Its ATR is between 0.5x and 2.0x current ATR.

The matched sample set is used only when it contains at least 15 observations. Otherwise the model falls back to all eligible historical samples.

3.4 Recency weighting

Recent examples receive exponentially higher weight:

weight(i) = exp(-0.006 * (latest index - historical index))

The matched probability receives 70% weight and the all-history probability receives 30% weight. With insufficient matched samples, only the all-history estimate is used.

3.5 Calibration

The raw historical estimate is passed through the piecewise-linear calibration curve in indicators.js. This reduces overconfidence, especially for rare large moves. The deployed curve ends at 77.1%, so even a raw 100% estimate is displayed as 77.1%.

5. Secondary Live Context Signal: RSI(14) Reversion

The dashboard calculates causal Wilder RSI(14) from the 1-minute closes:

RSI = 100 - 100 / (1 + average gain / average loss) UP candidate if RSI < 30 DOWN candidate if RSI > 70

The candidate is only displayed as a signal when the volatility regime is Normal. In Hot or Quiet regimes, the signal is suppressed and shown as “No signal (unreliable regime)” when RSI is extreme.

ConditionDisplayed label
RSI < 30 and Normal volatilityUP, with a hard-coded historical accuracy context of 58%.
RSI > 70 and Normal volatilityDOWN, with a hard-coded historical accuracy context of 62%.
RSI between 30 and 70No signal.
Extreme RSI outside Normal volatilityNo signal; regime deemed unreliable.

The RSI signal does not replace the target-touch probability. It is a separate mean-reversion context label. The dashboard intentionally does not create a call when conditions are ordinary.

6. Strategies Tested in Backtests

6.1 RSI(14) extreme reversion

At RSI below 30, predict UP; above 70, predict DOWN. The gated version only permits calls in the Normal ATR regime. The stricter robustness check also tests 20/80 thresholds and 5-minute and 15-minute horizons.

6.2 EMA9/EMA21 + MACD trend confirmation

Compute EMA(9), EMA(21), and MACD(12,26,9). The walk-forward implementation predicts UP when EMA9 > EMA21 and MACD histogram > 0; it predicts DOWN when both are below. In the standalone combo test, RSI above/below 50 is also required, making it a three-indicator confirmation strategy. No call is made when indicators disagree.

6.3 Bollinger Bands(20,2) mean reversion

Compute a 20-close simple moving average and bands at two population standard deviations. A close above the upper band predicts DOWN; a close below the lower band predicts UP; otherwise no call.

6.4 Historical momentum-only bias

The older bias experiment matched the current 20-bar momentum label to historical labels and estimated the next-minute up rate. It labeled Bullish above 55%, Bearish below 45%, and Neutral otherwise. This was not promoted as the live 15-minute engine because short-horizon momentum direction was close to chance.

6.5 Baselines

Walk-forward comparison includes Always UP, Always DOWN, and deterministic Random 50%. These are sanity checks, not proposed strategies.

9. Limitations and Correct Interpretation

  • Probability is conditional frequency: it estimates historical target touches under approximate similarity, not a future certainty.
  • Small target distance can look easy: the target percentage is measured from the live price and is sensitive to spread and microstructure.
  • Wicks count: a brief high or low touching the level is a success in the model.
  • Sample size matters: the table marks thin samples below 50 as less reliable.
  • Regime drift: crypto behavior can change; a 500-minute history is not a complete market model.
  • No execution controls: there is no order placement, stop, take-profit order, position sizing, leverage, or liquidation protection.
  • No news model: the local assistant does not currently fetch or interpret live news or external sentiment.
  • Backtest cost sensitivity: the reported candidate returns are negative after the chosen cost assumption, even where directional accuracy exceeds 50%.
  • Not financial advice: any use should include independent validation, defined risk, and a willingness to lose the entire position.

10. Practical Reading Checklist

  1. Confirm the current price and target direction.
  2. Check the target distance percentage. A farther target requires a larger historical excursion.
  3. Check whether trend agrees with the target direction.
  4. Check volatility regime. Hot and Quiet suppress the RSI reversion label.
  5. Prefer rows with adequate samples; treat thin samples cautiously.
  6. Read “Can Cross?” as a historical possibility filter, not an instruction to trade.
  7. Use a predefined risk limit and do not rely on the dashboard alone.
